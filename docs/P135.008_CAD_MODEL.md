# P135.008 — Parametric CAD model and assembly

Status: initial OCCT integration and the first three-limb core B-Rep skeleton are implemented and locally tested in Debug. The CAD tests cover all eight Butt-joint interfaces (top/bottom AB and BC to limbs A/B/C). The desktop 3D CAD tab remains a placeholder; assembly, bobbins, winding solids, viewport and exports are not implemented. The current core is a seven-solid compound, not a fused or manufacturing-ready core.

## Objective

Create a manufacturable, editable 3D assembly of the first physical product: a three-phase reactor used in the shunt passive LC filter. The same CAD architecture must later support VFD output sine-wave and du/dt filters.

The CAD model is downstream of the engineering domain model. Electrical and magnetic calculations remain authoritative for their own quantities; editing a CAD face or edge must never silently alter electrical parameters.

## Critical geometry rule

Magnetic equivalent parameters are not sufficient to reconstruct a unique solid model. In particular, effective magnetic area, window area and mean magnetic path do not uniquely define the physical cross-section, stack depth, window shape, corner radii, joints or manufacturing allowances.

The physical geometry contract must therefore carry explicit dimensions, including at minimum:

- core topology and assembly arrangement;
- independent limb widths/heights and yoke dimensions;
- stack depth and lamination/segment construction;
- window widths and heights;
- joint/step-lap and air-gap locations where applicable;
- bobbin dimensions, flanges and wall thickness;
- winding pack dimensions, layer/section arrangement and conductor build;
- turn/layer/section insulation build and required clearances;
- terminals, leads, mounting features and mechanical interfaces.

The solver's effective magnetic dimensions remain a separate model. Cross-checks may compare physical geometry-derived estimates with magnetic inputs, but must not silently replace either.

## CAD architecture

Keep OCCT types out of the core/domain public API. The CAD module owns the translation from validated engineering geometry to OCCT B-Rep shapes and assemblies.

- Geometry: validated parametric dimensions and feature definitions.
- Kernel: OCCT shape construction, topology checks and shape healing diagnostics.
- Assembly: component instances, placements, hierarchy, material/appearance metadata and BOM identity.
- Viewport: OCCT AIS presentation integrated into the Qt desktop page.
- Drawing: projection definitions, HLR visible/hidden edges, sections, annotations, dimensions and title block.
- Export: STEP/IGES for 3D; DXF for vector drawing geometry; PDF for plotted drawing sheets.

Use millimetres for CAD coordinates at the kernel boundary, with explicit conversion from SI domain quantities. Keep source engineering values in SI.

## Delivery sequence

1. P135.008.1 — Geometry contracts and validation. Define physical dimensions for the first three-phase reactor, units, tolerances, manufacturing metadata and validation diagnostics. No guessed dimensions and no inference from magnetic effective area alone.
2. P135.008.2 — OCCT core solids. Initial slice implemented: three limb solids and four yoke-segment solids in a validated compound. Limb placement now uses explicit limb and window widths, and domain validation checks the horizontal and vertical dimensional stack-ups. Air-gap cuts are implemented. Butt joints are checked at all eight interfaces. Step-Lap domain packets now carry an explicit extension direction and are validated for contiguous depth coverage and strict alternation; OCCT Step-Lap construction remains intentionally unsupported until the packet-to-solid geometry is implemented and tested.
3. P135.008.3 — Bobbin and winding solids. Generate bobbin, winding packs, insulation envelopes and terminals from explicit winding construction data.
4. P135.008.4 — Assembly and BOM. Place core, bobbins, windings, clamps and mounting parts; assign stable part IDs and generate a structured BOM.
5. P135.008.5 — Qt viewport and 3D exchange. Display selectable/shaded assembly in the CAD tab; export/import STEP and export IGES with round-trip checks.
6. P135.010 — Manufacturing drawings. Generate front/top/side views, sections, dimensions, tolerances, notes, winding/connection tables and BOM; export DXF and PDF. Drawing dimensions must be traceable to named geometry parameters.

## Acceptance criteria

- Same input model produces deterministic geometry and stable part identities.
- Invalid or incomplete dimensions produce explicit diagnostics; no fabricated defaults.
- Every generated solid is checked for null/invalid topology and positive volume; the complete assembly compound is checked for valid topology. Pairwise component intersections may contain shared contact faces but must have zero common volume.
- All eight Butt-joint interfaces are checked for coplanarity and contact across the stack depth; outer limbs use full-width contacts, while the middle limb is split into complementary half-width contacts at its centre plane. Step-Lap packet directions are explicit and adjacent packets must alternate; CAD must reject Step-Lap until actual interleaved solids are generated.
- Assembly bounding boxes and mass properties are available only when material/density data is verified.
- STEP round-trip preserves component count and shape validity within declared tolerances.
- Drawing dimensions reference model parameters and are not manually duplicated constants.
- Missing manufacturing data is reported as NOT_VERIFIED; a visually plausible model is not considered a manufacturing-ready design.

## Dependencies and scope boundaries

OCCT is the selected geometry kernel and is integrated through the isolated Project135Cad target using the pinned vcpkg opencascade dependency. Domain contracts remain free of OCCT types; core-only builds must remain independent of the CAD kernel.

FEM remains a separate analysis subsystem consuming the same physical geometry. CAD shape validity is not FEM mesh validation, and neither substitutes for standards or manufacturing review.

## Step-Lap packet contract

Each Step-Lap packet explicitly records its stack-depth interval, overlap into the limb, overlap into the yoke, and `StepLapPacketDirection` (`LimbIntoYoke` or `YokeIntoLimb`). Packets must be supplied in increasing stack-depth order, cover the shared depth continuously without overlap, and adjacent packets must alternate direction. Limb and yoke members must have equal physical stack depth because this contract has no independent depth-offset datum. `overlapIntoLimb` is bounded by the clear window height at that interface, and `overlapIntoYoke` by the corresponding yoke thickness; both are measured normal to the nominal butt plane. This is a project-level geometric convention for describing the packet sequence, not a claim that a complete manufacturing-standard Step-Lap profile has been implemented. The OCCT builder continues to return `UnsupportedConfiguration` for Step-Lap; it must not silently substitute a Butt joint. A later CAD milestone must define and test the exact per-packet solid extents and component splitting before enabling it.
